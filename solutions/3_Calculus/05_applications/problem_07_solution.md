### Essential Formulas

1. **Exponential Growth Differential Equation:**
   A quantity grows at a rate proportional to its current size if:

   $$
   \frac{dN}{dt} = k N(t)
   $$

   The general solution is the exponential model:

   $$
   N(t) = N_0 e^{kt}
   $$

   where $N_0 = N(0)$ is the initial value and $k > 0$ is the continuous growth rate.

2. **Chain Rule for Exponential Functions:**

   $$
   \frac{d}{dt}[e^{kt}] = k e^{kt}
   $$

3. **Natural Logarithm Inversion:**

   $$
   e^u = M \iff u = \ln M \qquad (M > 0)
   $$

---

### Problem Statement

Assume the model

$$
N(t)=N_0e^{kt},\qquad k>0.
$$

1. Verify by differentiation that the model satisfies the relation $N'(t)=kN(t)$.
2. Find the population doubling time.
3. Explain why the doubling time does not depend on $N_0$.

> **Why this problem:** it shows the connection between the exponential function and a growth rate proportional to the current value, without introducing a separate technique for solving differential equations.

---

### Solution

**Step 1: Verify the differential relation by differentiation**

Given the exponential population function:

$$
N(t) = N_0 e^{kt}
$$

Differentiate $N(t)$ with respect to time $t$:

$$
N'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\left[N_0 e^{kt}\right] = N_0 \frac{d}{dt}[e^{kt}]
$$

Apply the chain rule with inner function $u(t) = kt$, whose derivative is $k$:

$$
N'(t) = N_0 \cdot \left(e^{kt} \cdot k\right) = k \left(N_0 e^{kt}\right)
$$

Substitute the original formula $N(t) = N_0 e^{kt}$:

$$
N'(t) = k N(t)
$$

This verifies that the instantaneous rate of change of the population is directly proportional to its current population size, with proportionality constant $k$.

---

**Step 2: Find the population doubling time $T_d$**

Let $T_d$ denote the doubling time, the time required for the population to reach twice its initial size:

$$
N(T_d) = 2 N_0
$$

Substitute $t = T_d$ into the exponential model:

$$
N_0 e^{k T_d} = 2 N_0
$$

Assuming an initial population $N_0 > 0$, divide both sides by $N_0$:

$$
e^{k T_d} = 2
$$

Take the natural logarithm of both sides:

$$
\ln\left(e^{k T_d}\right) = \ln 2
$$

$$
k T_d = \ln 2
$$

Solve for $T_d$:

$$
T_d = \frac{\ln 2}{k} \approx \frac{0.693147}{k}
$$

---

**Step 3: Explain why the doubling time does not depend on $N_0$**

1. **Algebraic Cancellation:**
   In the equation $N_0 e^{k T_d} = 2 N_0$, the initial population $N_0$ appears as an identical multiplicative scaling factor on both sides. Dividing by $N_0$ eliminates it completely, leaving $e^{k T_d} = 2$, which depends solely on the growth constant $k$.
2. **Proportional Growth and Constant Per-Capita Rate:**
   The differential relation $N'(t) = k N(t)$ can be rewritten as:

   $$
   \frac{N'(t)}{N(t)} = k
   $$

   This shows that the *relative (per-capita) growth rate* is constant throughout time. Every single individual contributes to population growth at the exact same rate.
   * Doubling a population of $100$ individuals requires adding $100$ new members, generated by $100$ parents.
   * Doubling a population of $1{,}000{,}000$ individuals requires adding $1{,}000{,}000$ new members, generated by $1{,}000{,}000$ parents.
   Because the ratio of new individuals to existing producers is identical ($1:1$) at any scale, the time required to double is strictly invariant with respect to initial size.

---

### Final Answer

1. **Verification:**

   $$
   N'(t) = \frac{d}{dt}[N_0 e^{kt}] = k N_0 e^{kt} = k N(t)
   $$

2. **Doubling Time:**

   $$
   T_d = \frac{\ln 2}{k} \approx \frac{0.693}{k}
   $$

3. **Independence from $N_0$:**
   $N_0$ cancels out algebraically from $N_0 e^{k T_d} = 2 N_0$. Geometrically and biologically, because the per-capita growth rate $\frac{N'(t)}{N(t)} = k$ is constant, any population doubles in the same time interval regardless of whether it starts small or large.
